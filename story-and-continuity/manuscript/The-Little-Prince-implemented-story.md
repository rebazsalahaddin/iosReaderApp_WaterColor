## Chapter 1 of 27 · My First Drawing

### Scene 1 of 6 — A Picture in a Book

*(Page 1 of 4 — chapter opening page: the title “My First Drawing” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 4)*

When I was six, I found a picture I couldn't stop looking at. It was in a book about the forest: a huge snake swallowing an animal.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

The book said these snakes swallowed their food whole. After a meal, they couldn't move. They slept for six months!

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Six months! I kept thinking about that snake and all the adventures in the forest. At last, I took my pencil and began to draw.

### Scene 2 of 6 — Only a Hat

*(Page 1 of 3)*

I called it Drawing Number One. To me, it was wonderful. Now I wanted to see what the grown-ups thought.

*(Page 2 of 3)*

"Are you frightened?" I asked. "Frightened? It's only a hat," they said.

*(Page 3 of 3)*

A hat? It wasn't a hat at all. My snake had an elephant inside! But the grown-ups could only see the outside.

### Scene 3 of 6 — I Put Down My Pencil

*(Page 1 of 4)*

So I drew another picture. This time, I showed the inside of the snake, with the elephant in its big round body. Drawing Number Two.

*(Page 2 of 4)*

Now they could see the elephant. I waited. "Put those snakes away," they said. "Study geography, history, math, and grammar instead."

*(Page 3 of 4)*

I was only six, and already I was giving up my life as an artist. I put down my pencil.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Grown-ups need so many explanations. And when you're six, explaining everything, again and again, is very tiring.

### Scene 4 of 6 — Up in the Sky

*(Page 1 of 3)*

Years passed. I chose a different life and learned to fly planes. Since then, I've flown over many parts of the world.

*(Page 2 of 3)*

And yes, geography has helped. One look, and I know: China or Arizona. That's useful when you're lost in the dark.

*(Page 3 of 3)*

I've met many grown-ups in different countries. I've listened to all their important talk. And I still remember why I put down my pencil.

### Scene 5 of 6 — The Same Answer

*(Page 1 of 3)*

But sometimes I meet someone and think, "Perhaps this one will understand." I still have Drawing Number One. So I show it to them.

*(Page 2 of 3)*

I wait for their answer. "A hat," they say. Always a hat.

*(Page 3 of 3)*

After that, I say nothing more about snakes, forests, or stars. We talk about work instead. About money. About clothes.

### Scene 6 of 6 — Someone to Talk To

*(Page 1 of 2)*

And now they're pleased with me. Such a sensible man! We understand each other so well. At least, that's what they think.

*(Page 2 of 2)*

There were people all around me. But no one I could really talk to.

## Chapter 2 of 27 · Draw Me a Sheep

### Scene 1 of 4 — Alone in the Sahara

*(Page 1 of 4 — chapter opening page: the title “Draw Me a Sheep” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 4)*

Six years ago, my plane broke down in the Sahara Desert. There was no one with me. No passengers. No one to help with the engine.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

I had to repair it myself. My water could last only a week. Out there, getting the engine to work meant staying alive.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

That first night, I slept on the sand, a thousand miles from any town. A sailor alone in the middle of the ocean could hardly feel farther from home.

### Scene 2 of 4 — A Voice at Sunrise

*(Page 1 of 6)*

At sunrise, a small voice woke me. "Please, draw me a sheep." I sat up. "What?" "Draw me a sheep."

*(Page 2 of 6)*

A little boy stood there, watching me very seriously. He did not look lost, hungry, thirsty, or afraid. But there was nothing around us except sand.

*(Page 3 of 6)*

I have tried to draw him for you. My picture is not nearly as lovely as he was. Remember, the grown-ups stopped my drawing lessons when I was six.

*(Page 4 of 6)*

"What are you doing here?" He answered slowly, as if nothing could be more important. "Please. Draw me a sheep."

*(Page 5 of 6)*

There I was, with a broken plane and hardly any water. But I took out my paper and pen. What else could I do with such a strange little visitor?

*(Page 6 of 6)*

"I studied geography, history, math, and grammar," I said. "I don't know how to draw." "That doesn't matter. Draw me a sheep."

### Scene 3 of 4 — Three Sheep That Would Not Do

*(Page 1 of 5)*

I had never drawn a sheep. So I drew the picture I knew best: my snake from the outside. "No, no! I don't want an elephant inside a snake!"

*(Page 2 of 5)*

"That snake is dangerous, and the elephant is much too big. Where I live, everything is very small. I need a sheep." At last, someone could see my elephant.

*(Page 3 of 5)*

I tried to draw a sheep. The little prince studied it. "No. This one looks sick. Please draw another."

*(Page 4 of 5)*

I tried again. This time he smiled. "That's a ram. Look at its horns." So I began a third drawing.

*(Page 5 of 5)*

"This one is too old," he said. "I want a sheep that will live a long time." Three drawings. Still no sheep.

### Scene 4 of 4 — The Sheep in the Box

*(Page 1 of 4)*

My engine was waiting. I was losing patience. I drew a box, with a few holes in its side. "There. Your sheep is inside."

*(Page 2 of 4)*

His whole face brightened. "Yes! That's just what I wanted. Do you think it needs much grass?" "Why?" "Because my home is very small."

*(Page 3 of 4)*

"There will be enough," I said. "I've given you a very small sheep." He bent over the picture. "Not so very small. Look! It's asleep."

*(Page 4 of 4)*

And that was how I met the little prince.

## Chapter 3 of 27 · Where Do You Come From?

### Scene 1 of 3 — The Plane

*(Page 1 of 5 — chapter opening page: the title “Where Do You Come From?” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 5)*

The little prince had question after question. My questions were another matter. He hardly seemed to hear them. I learned his story a little at a time.

*(Page 3 of 5)*

"What's that thing?" he asked when he saw my plane. "It isn't just a thing. It flies. It's my airplane." I was rather proud to tell him that.

*(Page 4 of 5)*

I won't try to draw the plane here. That is much too difficult for me. But the little prince was looking at the real one.

*(Page 5 of 5)*

"What? You fell out of the sky?" "Yes." "Oh, that's funny!" He laughed. I did not. I like people to take my troubles seriously.

### Scene 2 of 3 — Another Planet

*(Page 1 of 3)*

"Then you came from the sky too! Which planet is yours?" Too? That little word caught my attention. "Do you come from another planet?"

*(Page 2 of 3)*

He did not answer. He kept looking at my plane. "You can't have come from very far away in that." Then he grew quiet.

*(Page 3 of 3)*

He took the sheep's picture from his pocket and looked at it for a long time. I could wait no longer. "Where is your home? Where will you take your sheep?"

### Scene 3 of 3 — A House for the Sheep

*(Page 1 of 4)*

"The good thing about this box," he said at last, "is that the sheep can sleep in it at night." "Yes. And I can draw some string and a post for the daytime."

*(Page 2 of 4)*

"Then you can tie your sheep to the post." "Tie him up? What a strange idea!" "But he could walk away and get lost."

*(Page 3 of 4)*

"Where could he go?" "Anywhere. Straight ahead." The little prince laughed again. "It doesn't matter. Everything is so small where I live."

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Then his voice changed. There was a little sadness in it now. "On my planet, you can't go very far in a straight line."

## Chapter 4 of 27 · B-612 Is His Home

### Scene 1 of 5 — A Planet With a Number

*(Page 1 of 4 — chapter opening page: the title “B-612 Is His Home” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 4)*

So my little friend came from a planet hardly bigger than a house. That did not surprise me as much as you might think.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

We know the names of Earth, Jupiter, Mars, and Venus. But there are many smaller worlds too. Some are hard to see even through a telescope.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

An astronomer finds one and gives it a number instead of a name. Something like Asteroid 325. I believe my friend's home was B-612.

### Scene 2 of 5 — The Same Discovery

*(Page 1 of 4)*

A Turkish astronomer saw B-612 through his telescope in 1909. He told a meeting of other astronomers about it. Nobody believed him. They disliked his clothes.

*(Page 2 of 4)*

Later, a Turkish leader ordered people to wear European clothes. Anyone who refused could be put to death. A cruel rule about clothes, not about the stars.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

In 1920, the astronomer explained his discovery again, wearing European clothes. This time, everyone believed him. The little planet had not changed at all.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Grown-ups can be like that.

### Scene 3 of 5 — What Makes a Friend Real

*(Page 1 of 6)*

Tell a grown-up about a new friend. Will they ask how his voice sounds? Which games he loves? Whether he collects butterflies?

*(Page 2 of 6)*

More often, they ask his age, how many brothers he has, how much he weighs, and how much money his father earns. Then they think they know him.

*(Page 3 of 6)*

Tell them about a pink house, with flowers in its windows and birds on its roof. They may not see it in their minds at all.

*(Page 4 of 6)*

Tell them it costs twenty thousand dollars, and they cry, "What a beautiful house!" The price has done what the flowers could not.

*(Page 5 of 6)*

I could tell them that the little prince laughed, that he was lovely, that he wanted a sheep. What better reason to believe he was real?

*(Page 6 of 6)*

But a grown-up may only smile at me. Say "Asteroid B-612," and the questions stop. We children must be patient with grown-ups.

### Scene 4 of 5 — Once Upon a Time

*(Page 1 of 4)*

I wish I could begin like this: "Once upon a time, a little prince lived on a very small planet. He needed a sheep."

*(Page 2 of 4)*

For people who understand life, that is enough. Numbers are not what make a story true. But please, don't hurry through this one.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

It has been six years since my friend went away with his sheep. Remembering him hurts. Forgetting him would hurt in another way.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Not everyone has had a friend. I don't want to become a grown-up who remembers only numbers. So I have bought paints and pencils again.

### Scene 5 of 5 — Drawing Him Again

*(Page 1 of 3)*

Drawing is difficult after all these years. Sometimes I make him too tall, sometimes too short. I am not quite sure about the colors of his clothes.

*(Page 2 of 3)*

I do my best. I may get more important things wrong too. The little prince did not explain everything. Perhaps he thought I understood as he did.

*(Page 3 of 3)*

But I cannot see a sheep through the sides of a box. Perhaps I am a little like the grown-ups after all. I have grown older too.

## Chapter 5 of 27 · Look Out for the Baobabs!

### Scene 1 of 4 — A Question About Sheep

*(Page 1 of 6 — chapter opening page: the title “Look Out for the Baobabs!” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 6)*

Each day, a few more words brought me closer to his little world. On the third day, the sheep led us to the baobabs.

*(Page 3 of 6)*

"Sheep eat little bushes, don't they?" "Yes." "Good! Then they eat baobabs too." He sounded pleased. I was confused.

*(Page 4 of 6)*

"Baobabs aren't little bushes. They're enormous trees. A whole group of elephants couldn't eat one." The little prince laughed.

*(Page 5 of 6)*

"We'd have to put the elephants on top of each other! But before a baobab becomes big, it starts small." "True. Why do you want the small ones eaten?"

*(Page 6 of 6)*

"Oh, you know!" He thought the answer was obvious. I had to work it out for myself.

### Scene 2 of 4 — What Sleeps Under the Ground

*(Page 1 of 4)*

His planet had helpful plants and dangerous plants. Their seeds lay hidden in the dark ground. You could not see which ones were waiting there.

*(Page 2 of 4)*

Then one seed would wake. A small green plant would reach toward the sun. At first, even a dangerous plant could look safe.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

A rose or a little vegetable plant could stay. A baobab could not. He had to recognize it and pull it up while it was small.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Wait too long, and the roots would spread through the whole planet. Too many baobabs could break that little world apart.

### Scene 3 of 4 — A Planet Needs Care

*(Page 1 of 3)*

"You need discipline," he told me. "First you wash yourself in the morning. Then you look after your planet."

*(Page 2 of 3)*

"You pull up the baobabs as soon as you can tell them from the young roses. It's easy work. You just have to remember to do it."

*(Page 3 of 3)*

"Make a good drawing for the children at home. It could help them if they travel one day. Some jobs can wait. Baobabs can't."

### Scene 4 of 4 — The Planet That Waited

*(Page 1 of 4)*

"I knew a planet with a lazy man on it. He left three little bushes alone." Three little bushes. That was all it took.

*(Page 2 of 4)*

I drew that planet as the little prince described it. I don't usually like giving lessons. But this time I must say it: children, watch out for the baobabs!

*(Page 3 of 4)*

My friends and I had lived close to that danger without knowing it. That is why I worked so hard on this picture. I wanted them to see.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

Perhaps you wonder why my other drawings are not so good. I tried. But this was a picture I felt I simply had to get right.

## Chapter 6 of 27 · Forty-Four Sunsets

### Scene 1 of 2 — Not Yet

*(Page 1 of 4 — chapter opening page: the title “Forty-Four Sunsets” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 4)*

Little by little, I began to understand his sadness. For a long time, watching the sun go down had been his quiet pleasure. He told me on the fourth morning.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

"I love sunsets. Let's watch one now." "We have to wait." "Wait for what?" "For the sun to go down."

*(Page 4 of 4)*

He looked surprised. Then he laughed at himself. "I keep thinking I'm at home!"

### Scene 2 of 2 — A Chair and a Small World

*(Page 1 of 4)*

Here on Earth, evening can reach France while it is still noon in part of America. But you cannot hurry across the ocean in a minute to catch it.

*(Page 2 of 4)*

On his small planet, there was no ocean to cross. He only had to move his chair a little. Then he could watch the sun go down again.

*(Page 3 of 4)*

"One day, I watched forty-four sunsets." A little later, he added: "Sunsets are lovely when you're sad."

*(Page 4 of 4)*

"Were you very sad that day? The day of the forty-four sunsets?" The little prince did not answer.

## Chapter 7 of 27 · Important Things

### Scene 1 of 4 — What Are Thorns For?

*(Page 1 of 7 — chapter opening page: the title “Important Things” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 7)*

On the fifth day, another question about the sheep brought me closer to his secret. "If a sheep eats plants, does it eat flowers too?"

*(Page 3 of 7)*

"It eats whatever it can reach." "Even flowers with thorns?" "Even those." "Then what are thorns for?"

*(Page 4 of 7)*

I didn't know. A bolt was stuck in my engine, and I could not turn it. The damage was serious. I had very little water left. I was afraid.

*(Page 5 of 7)*

"What are thorns for?" Once he asked a question, he kept asking. I answered without thinking. "Nothing. Flowers grow them just to be unpleasant."

*(Page 6 of 7)*

"I don't believe you! Flowers are weak. They grow a few thorns and think those will keep them safe." I was still looking at the bolt.

*(Page 7 of 7)*

If it would not turn, I thought, I would hit it with my hammer. The little prince began again. "So you really think flowers..."

### Scene 2 of 4 — Like the Grown-Ups

*(Page 1 of 5)*

"No! I don't think anything! I said the first thing that came into my head. Can't you see I'm busy with important things?"

*(Page 2 of 5)*

"Important things?" He looked at my dirty hands, my hammer, the ugly engine. "You sound just like the grown-ups."

*(Page 3 of 5)*

That made me sorry. But he had not finished. "You mix everything up. You don't understand what is important."

*(Page 4 of 5)*

"I know a man who never smells flowers or looks at stars. He has never loved anyone. He only counts. All day he says, 'I'm a serious man!'"

*(Page 5 of 5)*

"But he isn't a man. He's a mushroom!" "A mushroom?" "A mushroom!"

### Scene 3 of 4 — One Flower Among the Stars

*(Page 1 of 5)*

"Flowers have grown thorns for millions of years. Sheep have eaten them all that time. Isn't it important to understand why the flowers keep trying?"

*(Page 2 of 5)*

"And suppose there is one flower, the only one like her anywhere. She lives on my planet. One morning, a sheep could eat her in a single bite."

*(Page 3 of 5)*

"The sheep wouldn't even know what it had done. And you think that isn't important?" His voice was shaking now.

*(Page 4 of 5)*

"If you love one flower on one star, you can look at all the stars and feel happy. Somewhere up there, your flower is alive."

*(Page 5 of 5)*

"But if a sheep eats her, all those stars go dark. And you think that isn't important!" Then he could say no more. He began to cry.

### Scene 4 of 4 — My Friend Was Crying

*(Page 1 of 3)*

Night had come. I put down my tools. For a moment, I forgot the engine, the bolt, even how thirsty I was. My little friend was crying.

*(Page 2 of 3)*

I took him in my arms. "I'll draw a muzzle for your sheep. And a fence for your flower. I'll..."

*(Page 3 of 3)*

I did not know what else to promise. I wanted to reach him, but I did not know the way. When someone is crying, it can be hard to find a way to them.

## Chapter 8 of 27 · The Flower

### Scene 1 of 4 — Something New

*(Page 1 of 5 — chapter opening page: the title “The Flower” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 5)*

Soon I learned more about his flower. Before she came, the flowers on his planet were simple things. They opened in the morning and died by evening.

*(Page 3 of 5)*

Then a strange seed arrived. The little prince watched its plant carefully. Could it be one of those dangerous trees?

*(Page 4 of 5)*

But it stopped growing taller. A great bud appeared instead. Something beautiful was getting ready inside it.

*(Page 5 of 5)*

The flower took her time. She chose her colors. She arranged each petal. She was not going to appear with everything in a mess. Days passed.

### Scene 2 of 4 — Born With the Sun

*(Page 1 of 4)*

One morning, just as the sun rose, she opened. "Oh! I'm hardly awake. My petals are still a mess." After all those days of getting ready!

*(Page 2 of 4)*

"You're beautiful." "Am I not? And I was born with the sun."

*(Page 3 of 4)*

She was rather proud of herself. He could not stop looking at her.

*(Page 4 of 4)*

"I think it's time for breakfast. Perhaps you could remember my needs?" He hurried to bring her fresh water.

### Scene 3 of 4 — Four Thorns

*(Page 1 of 6)*

But loving her was not always easy. One day, she showed him her four thorns. "Let the tigers come with their claws!"

*(Page 2 of 6)*

"There aren't any tigers here. And tigers don't eat plants." "I am not just any plant." "I'm sorry."

*(Page 3 of 6)*

"I'm not afraid of tigers. But cold wind frightens me. Do you have a screen to keep the wind off?" Afraid of a little wind! What a strange flower.

*(Page 4 of 6)*

"Put me under glass at night. It's very cold here. Where I came from..." She stopped. She had arrived as a seed. What other home could she remember?

*(Page 5 of 6)*

She gave a little cough. "My screen?"

*(Page 6 of 6)*

"I was just going to get it." She coughed again, making him feel he had done something wrong.

### Scene 4 of 4 — Too Young to Understand

*(Page 1 of 5)*

He loved her, but he began to doubt her words. Small things made him unhappy. Later, he spoke to me very differently.

*(Page 2 of 5)*

"I listened to what she said and forgot to notice what she gave me. Her beauty. Her lovely smell. She filled my whole planet."

*(Page 3 of 5)*

"All that talk about claws! It was only her way of trying to be brave. I took it so seriously."

*(Page 4 of 5)*

"I needed to look at her actions, not just her words. There was love behind those little tricks. I didn't understand. I should never have left her."

*(Page 5 of 5)*

"I was too young to know how to love her."

## Chapter 9 of 27 · Goodbye

### Scene 1 of 2 — Caring for His Home

*(Page 1 of 6 — chapter opening page: the title “Goodbye” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 6)*

I think he left with a group of wild birds as they traveled across the sky. But first, on the morning he left, he put his planet in order.

*(Page 3 of 6)*

He cleaned his two burning volcanoes. They were useful for warming breakfast. On his little world, careful cleaning kept their fires burning gently.

*(Page 4 of 6)*

The third volcano was cold. He cleaned that one too. "You never know." On Earth, of course, our volcanoes are much too big for such housework.

*(Page 5 of 6)*

He pulled up the last little baobabs. He thought he was leaving forever. Suddenly, he loved each familiar job.

*(Page 6 of 6)*

He watered his flower and lifted her glass cover. It was the last time. He wanted to cry. "Goodbye." She said nothing.

### Scene 2 of 2 — What She Could Not Say Before

*(Page 1 of 7)*

He said goodbye again. She coughed, though she had no cold. "I've been silly. I am sorry. Try to be happy."

*(Page 2 of 7)*

He stood still, holding the glass. He had expected complaints, not kindness. "Of course I love you. I should have told you."

*(Page 3 of 7)*

"We've both been silly. But never mind that now. Be happy. And leave the glass. I don't want it."

*(Page 4 of 7)*

"But the wind..." "The cool air will do me good. I'm a flower." "But the animals..."

*(Page 5 of 7)*

"Two or three caterpillars can stay if butterflies come later. Who else will visit me? You'll be far away."

*(Page 6 of 7)*

"As for bigger animals, I have my claws." She showed him her four thorns.

*(Page 7 of 7)*

"Don't wait like this. You've decided to go. Go." She did not want him to see her cry. She was such a proud flower.

## Chapter 10 of 27 · The King

### Scene 1 of 4 — Someone to Rule

*(Page 1 of 8 — chapter opening page: the title “The King” is displayed as the page)*

*(Page 2 of 8)*

Six small planets lay near his own: 325, 326, 327, 328, 329, and 330. The little prince decided to visit them. Perhaps he could learn something.

*(Page 3 of 8)*

A king lived on the first planet. His long purple coat, with white fur along its edges, spread around his great chair. "Ah! Someone for me to rule!"

*(Page 4 of 8)*

How could the king know him already? The little prince did not understand that, to a king, everyone is a person to rule. "Come closer. Let me see you."

*(Page 5 of 8)*

There was nowhere to sit. The king's coat covered almost everything. Tired from his journey, the little prince yawned. "You must not yawn before a king!"

*(Page 6 of 8)*

"I can't help it. I've traveled a long way without sleeping." "Then I order you to yawn. I haven't seen a yawn for years. Go on!"

*(Page 7 of 8)*

"Now I'm too nervous. I can't." "Then I order you to yawn sometimes, and sometimes to..."

*(Page 8 of 8)*

The king stopped. This was becoming difficult.

### Scene 2 of 4 — What People Can Do

*(Page 1 of 7)*

"May I sit down?" "I order you to sit."

*(Page 2 of 7)*

At last, the king moved his coat. He wanted everyone to obey, but he tried to give sensible orders.

*(Page 3 of 7)*

"If I tell a soldier to become a bird and he cannot, who is wrong? Me. I must ask people to do things they can do."

*(Page 4 of 7)*

"May I ask a question?" "I order you to ask." "What do you rule?" "Everything."

*(Page 5 of 7)*

The king pointed at his planet, the other planets, and the stars. "All of that? And the stars obey you?"

*(Page 6 of 7)*

"At once. I allow nothing else."

*(Page 7 of 7)*

Such power! With it, the little prince could watch seventy-two sunsets, or a hundred, or two hundred, without moving his chair. He thought of home.

### Scene 3 of 4 — Waiting for the Sun

*(Page 1 of 5)*

"Please order the sun to set." "Suppose I order a soldier to fly like a butterfly, write a sad play, or become a bird. If he fails, who is wrong?"

*(Page 2 of 5)*

"You are." "Exactly. If I order people into the sea, they will fight me. My orders must make sense. That is why people obey me."

*(Page 3 of 5)*

"And my sunset?" "You shall have it. When the time is right."

*(Page 4 of 5)*

He opened a thick book. "At about seven forty this evening. You'll see how well the sun obeys."

*(Page 5 of 5)*

The little prince yawned. He was missing his sunset, and this visit was getting boring. "There is nothing more for me to do here. I think I'll go."

### Scene 4 of 4 — A Job for the Prince

*(Page 1 of 8)*

"Don't go! I'll give you an important job." "What job?" "You can judge people." "But there is nobody here."

*(Page 2 of 8)*

"I haven't looked everywhere. I'm old. Walking tires me, and there's no room for a carriage." The little prince looked around the other side. Nobody.

*(Page 3 of 8)*

"Then judge yourself. That is much harder than judging other people. Do it well, and you will understand yourself." "But I can do that anywhere."

*(Page 4 of 8)*

"I think there is an old rat here. I hear him at night. You can order his death, then let him live each time. We have only one rat. We must keep him."

*(Page 5 of 8)*

"I don't want to order anyone's death. I would rather go." "No."

*(Page 6 of 8)*

The little prince was ready to leave, but he did not want to hurt the old king.

*(Page 7 of 8)*

"You could order me to leave within one minute. That would be a sensible order. The time seems right." The king said nothing.

*(Page 8 of 8)*

The little prince waited, then left. "You will speak for me in every place you visit!" The king still sounded very important. The little prince thought that grown-ups were very strange.

## Chapter 13 of 27 · Counting the Stars
